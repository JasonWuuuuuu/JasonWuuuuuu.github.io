// hello, world — Why I Started This Blog

Jason Wu · ⏱ ~5 min read

As a Ph.D. student who crossed over from electrical engineering into transportation, I keep getting asked: "You studied power engineering — why traffic?" My answers always felt rushed. This blog is half a place to put those thoughts down, and half an attempt to stretch those rushed answers into something complete.

Why start writing?

Once I started the Ph.D. I noticed something odd: the more research I did, the fewer people I could explain it to. Papers are written formally — but the "why I picked this direction", "where I got stuck for three months", "why we threw out the first approach" stories all get compressed into a single line of acknowledgements. Yet those flattened parts are exactly what would actually help someone else.

So instead of letting those notes moulder in some forgotten Obsidian vault, I figured I'd put them somewhere public — even if just one person stumbles in from a search, sees me say "I hit this bug too", and feels a little less alone, that's worth it.

Writing isn't transcribing what you already know — it's the act of figuring it out.

— someone I can't remember, but the line stuck

What will I write about?

Roughly four buckets:

  • Research notes — method walkthroughs and gotchas in lane-change interaction modeling, causal inference, trajectory planning.
  • Engineering in the wild — real problems from edge deployment, device selection, algorithm landing; reproducible when I can manage it.
  • Paper readings — short takes on papers worth remembering.
  • Ph.D. life — non-technical stuff: time management, mindset, talking with advisors, maybe some book notes.

Code block

A tiny Python example — the simplest possible car-following update:

import numpy as np

def krauss(v: float, v_desired: float, dt: float, a_max: float = 1.5) -> float:
    """Naive car-following velocity update."""
    # Accelerate toward desired speed, capped by a_max
    if v < v_desired:
        v_next = min(v + a_max * dt, v_desired)
    else:
        v_next = max(v - a_max * dt, v_desired)
    return max(0.0, v_next)

# Simulate 10 seconds
v, t = 0.0, 0.0
while t < 10.0:
    v = krauss(v, v_desired=20.0, dt=0.1)
    print(f"t={t:.1f}s  v={v:.2f} m/s")
    t += 0.1
